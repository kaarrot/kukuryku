# Five tract commits cut CPU 10%, then stall

The work is heading in the right direction on method and precision strategy, but toward a nearly exhausted pool of gains, and it has not yet moved the battery and heat objective that motivated it. Per commit: the profiling tooling (b25e21e) is a cheap, correct enabler; the stage-1 A55 kernel default (5c79700) and the small-matmul pool bypass (8f4cf95) are right-direction wins; the AdaIN activation and FP16 boundary fusion (c250b05) and the contiguous Resize (cd78298) are correct and bit-identical but marginal returns for their complexity. In warm, alternating A/B runs on the Galaxy S10e, process CPU fell **about 10%** (roughly 39.2 s to 35.4 s per 22.8 s of audio; about 8-12% once chained-campaign error is allowed for). About six of those ten points are cheap little-core seconds, so gold-core CPU, which dominates energy, fell only **about 6%**. No joules, sustained-thermal or time-to-first-audio figure exists for any commit, and the energy tests run so far (same-binary spreads of 7-32%) cannot resolve the 3-5% effect that the CPU numbers imply. External evidence supports FP16 with FP32 islands and argues against INT8, and shows upstream tract 0.23.x already shipping analogues of the A55 selection, small-matmul threshold and nearest-upsample changes. At HEAD, stage 2 is **78% GEMM running at about 65% of the A76 FP16 peak**; non-GEMM work left is at most about 12% of gold CPU, so realistic further op-level recovery is 2-5%. The next steps are to make energy measurable, test a lower gold clock, spike stock tract 0.23.9 and attack first-audio latency, before shaving any more ops.

## Three commits earn their keep and two return less than they cost

The five commits execute, in order, the ranked list from the 7d34861 battery review (fusion, kernel benchmark, tiny-dispatch, Resize), which itself promised "measured incremental improvements" and "no evidence ... for a dramatic battery-life multiplier" ([battery-review-7d34861.md](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/battery-review-7d34861.md)). Judged against that modest promise, the results sort cleanly.

| Commit | Verdict | Measured effect (paired A/B) | What drags it down |
|---|---|---|---|
| b25e21e profiling | Right direction (enabler) | No inference change; per-node seconds reconcile exactly to wall time; fed three later investigations | Single-pass evidence; raw profiles are git-ignored, so doc links are dead in a clone |
| c250b05 AdaIN + Cast fusion | Marginal | Inference time -2.9% (4 silent pairs); CPU -0.2% to -0.55%, not significant; byte-identical output | About 450 fork lines tied to `sum_f32` alignment; gain is mostly a serial Cast moved into a parallel loop (inference) |
| 5c79700 A55 stage-1 default | Right direction; largest gain | CPU -5.7%, summed inference -10.1%; byte-identical output | Saved seconds are little-core seconds; compile-time Android gate, not CPU detection |
| 8f4cf95 small-matmul bypass | Right direction; best return per line | CPU -3.3% (gold -4.0%); wall only -1.1% | Threshold tuned on one phone; latency gain marginal |
| cd78298 contiguous Resize | Marginal | CPU -1.0% against a 1.06% ceiling | 20.6k added artifact lines and about 11 hours for 0.37 s per 22.8 s of audio |

**b25e21e** is the cheapest good decision in the series: 56 lines, off by default, timers that wrap only `eval`, and headline numbers (stage-2 matmul 72.5-74.5%, AdaIN-plus-Cast region 10.6-11.7%) that reproduce from the raw JSON. Its forecast was honest and correct, refusing to "justify promising a 10% inference or battery improvement" ([adain-fusion-investigation.md](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/adain-fusion-investigation.md)); realised stage-2 savings were 3.9-5.5%, about a third of the region's cost. Its one weakness is that it ranked fusion first partly on logical tensor traffic that never became saved CPU.

**c250b05** is the weakest return. It is well validated: six raw-FP32 comparisons across two voices and three speeds plus 14 WAV hashes matched byte for byte, and the validation doc itself says the commit "should not claim an energy reduction" ([adain-fusion-validation.md](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/adain-fusion-validation.md)). But the four-pair silent series gives a 2.9% inference-time gain with CPU down only 0.55% (t about -1.5), and the profile shows why: on the long sentence Cast time fell from 0.745 s to 0.291 s while the fused AdaIN-plus-activation ops cost slightly more than the ops they replaced. The likeliest mechanism, which is inference rather than measurement, is that the old Cast ran on one thread and the fused conversion now runs inside the row-parallel loop, so latency falls and energy does not. A roughly 40-line parallel-Cast patch from the same session cut inference time 3.75% in four of four pairs but raised CPU 0.33% and slowed stage 1, so it was shelved because it "did not reduce total CPU work" ([matmul-kernel-investigation.md](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/matmul-kernel-investigation.md)). The accept/shelve split between the two turns on CPU differences (-0.55% versus +0.33%) smaller than the 0.7-1.0% paired noise, so the decision rule discriminated below its own floor. The commit's most durable value is its parity checker and warm-playback harness, which every later commit imports.

**5c79700** is the right call on the evidence. It routes 172 stage-1 FP16 matrices to the existing `16x8_a55` tile (no assembly changed, 56 functional lines), cuts CPU 5.7% in four warm pairs with a same-executable control at -5.3%, and leaves output bit-identical ([warm-ab.json](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/profiling/matmul-c250b05/warm-ab.json)). Three caveats matter. About 70% of the dense-layer gain is available from the portable `16x8_gen` tile (-25.7% versus the old 64x3 tile, against -36.2% for the a55 tile); the A55-specific scheduling adds only 10-12 points. The a55 tile runs 43-50% slower than generic on the gold cores, so the default is safe only because stage 1 is pinned to the little cluster. And the 12% wall and 28% gap improvements are inflated by the benchmark's ascending sentence lengths, which put stage 1 on the critical path; on evenly sized prose stage 1 is hidden behind stage 2 and the gain shrinks to first-audio latency (inference from the committed profiles) ([matmul-kernel-investigation.md](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/matmul-kernel-investigation.md)).

**8f4cf95** is the best return per line: 45 production lines, no arithmetic change, CPU -3.28% in six of six pairs and -3.12% in an independent two-pair control. Gold-cluster voluntary context switches fell 86.9% (19,585 to 2,575), which says the saving is wasted wake/park coordination rather than removed compute ([warm-same-binary-analysis.json](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/profiling/tiny-dispatch-5c79700/warm-same-binary-analysis.json)). The 2^18-MAC cutoff rests on roughly two bracketing data points, but it is not delicate: no model geometry sits between 139,520 and 318,600 MACs, so any cutoff in that range routes identically ([tiny-dispatch-investigation.md](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/tiny-dispatch-investigation.md)). Which dispatches produced the saving was never recorded; the LSTM-Scan attribution is inference.

**cd78298** captured essentially its whole ceiling. Resize is 2.6-3.1% of stage-2 wall time; the fast path cuts Resize op time 86-89% and CPU 1.04% (six of six pairs, -1.53% in a four-pair control), against a 1.06% ceiling ([warm-bounded-analysis.json](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/profiling/resize-8f4cf95/warm-bounded-analysis.json)). The code is exact and fully guarded, but the commit adds about 20,600 lines of measurement artifacts for a 1% result. The review that seeded the series already ranked Resize last, noting that removing its entire cost would remove only "about 1.7% of total inference time". There is nothing left to gain here.

## CPU seconds fell about 10%, but only 6% of that landed on the gold cores

Chaining the four warm-harness effects gives a product of 0.8986, a **10.1% CPU reduction**; comparing the fused baseline (39.16 s per request) with HEAD (35.23-35.38 s) directly gives 9.7-10.0%. The per-commit paired-t intervals, derived from the committed per-round JSON, are tight enough that every CPU effect except fusion excludes zero.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| 5c79700 | CPU s | -5.7% | [-6.2, -5.2], n=4; control -5.3% (n=2) | [warm-ab.json](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/profiling/matmul-c250b05/warm-ab.json) |

| 8f4cf95 | CPU s | -3.3% | [-3.8, -2.7], n=6; control -3.1% (n=2) | [warm-same-binary-analysis.json](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/profiling/tiny-dispatch-5c79700/warm-same-binary-analysis.json) |

| cd78298 | Summed inference / synthesis wall | -0.6% / -0.2% | [-1.7, +0.5] / [-1.4, +0.9] | same |

I spot-checked the 8f4cf95 and cd78298 CPU means and the 5c79700 run list against the committed JSON; they match the notes. Three sources of error sit outside the intervals. The baselines come from separate campaigns, and the identical configuration (dispatch policy on, generic Resize) measured 1.573 and 1.568 CPU s per audio-second across two campaigns, a 0.3% drift, but 2.0% drift in summed inference and 2.6% in synthesis wall, about as large as the latency effects being chained. The headline "-14% inference, -15% wall RTF" (infer RTF 0.934 to 0.790, wall RTF 0.723 to 0.617) therefore compares a cold single CLI run with warm-daemon runs and is a bracket, not a like-for-like measurement. And every run is short, cool and unloaded: governor off, discard sink, three pinned gold cores, ending at 47-67 C, whereas the user's heat complaint lives at 80-85 C reached within 25-45 s of sustained load ([battery-and-heat.md](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/battery-and-heat.md)). The only long real-playback run, on the fusion commit, peaked at 80-85 C for both binaries.

The cluster split changes the meaning of the headline. The 5c79700 saving of about 2.2 CPU-seconds equals its drop in summed stage-1 inference time, so it is single-threaded little-core work; gold-thread CPU fell from 31.22 s to 29.44 s across the dispatch and Resize commits, **-5.7%**, plus about 0.4% from fusion. If a gold CPU-second costs 2-8 times a little one and CPU compute is about 2 of the 3.2 J per audio-second seen in whole-phone data, the implied whole-phone saving is 3-5%, or 0.10-0.15 J per audio-second. Both inputs are assumptions: neither the repo nor the web yields a per-core A55 versus A76 power figure. That effect is far below the energy method's resolution. Same-binary fusion pairs differed by 21% and 32% in J per audio-second with idle power wandering 0.72-1.72 W ([adain-fusion-validation.md](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/adain-fusion-validation.md)), and the earlier 11f792c study saw a real 5-6% CPU cut vanish in the battery ([battery-and-heat.md](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/battery-and-heat.md)). "No energy gain" in the project memory therefore means "none demonstrated and none resolvable", not "none exists". Time-to-first-audio, the user-visible latency for editor use, is not recorded at HEAD; the closest proxy, stage-1 plus stage-2 op time of one profiled run per sentence, fell 8%, 14% and 17% for short, medium and long sentences, with single-op times moving several percent between near-identical builds.

### Where the notes disagree, and which number to trust

Four disagreements affect interpretation. First, "about 3% faster" for fusion holds for four silent pairs (-2.93%) but weakens to -1.95% in sustained playback, where one pair shows +0.1%; quote 2-3% as latency, not work. The memory's "stage 1 +0.14 s slower" recomputes to +0.11 s (+1.4%) and is within stage-1 spread. Second, the parallel-Cast experiment is recorded as "-0.2 s net" in memory, which matches the JSON-profiled run (-0.28 s, n=3), but the plain run shows -0.81 s (-3.75%), recomputed independently in [cast-parallel-claim-verification.md](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/cast-parallel-claim-verification.md). Third, the "40 raw tensors" parity figure overstates the evidence for the stage-2-only commits: only the 10 stage-2 waveforms can differ under 8f4cf95 or cd78298, and the fusion's gating is stage 2 as well; for 5c79700 all 40 are relevant. Fourth, the detectability of energy effects depends on which noise estimate is used: about 6% from the 11f792c series (paired spread 0.3 J/s on about 4.6), +-7% per pair in the fusion's silent unplugged runs, and 21-32% in warm playback, which is why one note needs 200-800 pairs to see a 3-5% effect and another thinks 4-5 pairs reach a 6% floor. Until an A/A null run exists, the true floor is unknown. Finally, the evidence behind the fusion's 2.9% and the 0.2% energy series exists only in a temporary scratchpad and memory notes, not in the repo.

## External evidence backs the precision strategy and the method, not the op-shaving

The external record is sparse, so each claim below is as strong as its source. On precision, every Kokoro-specific report found supports the repo's FP16 choice and rejects INT8. On a Pixel 10 Pro, sherpa-onnx int8 ran at RTF about 0.62 against 0.48-0.51 for fp32, with steady tones at the iSTFT frame rate 15-20 dB above the noise floor and intermittent rail-pinned garbage ([sherpa-onnx #3754](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/issues/3754)); the root cause was dynamic quantization of the STFT convolutions producing 0/0 NaN phases ([PR #4087](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/pull/4087)). On an iPhone 15 int8 took 39.13 s against 19.41 s for fp32 ([sherpa-onnx #2374](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/issues/2374)), and kokoro-onnx measured spectral similarity 0.999 for fp16 but 0.916 for int8 ([PR #198](https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx/pull/198)). TensorFlow's XNNPACK FP16 work reports "close to 2X" over FP32 on ARMv8.2 phones ([TensorFlow blog](https://blog.tensorflow.org/2023/11/half-precision-inference-doubles-on-device-inference-performance.html)), and the LiteRT Kokoro port had to rescale the sine-source phase, swap `sum` for `mean` and raise epsilons to get correct FP16 audio ([litert-community/Kokoro-82M](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/Kokoro-82M)). The repo's FP32 islands (AdaIN/Snake arithmetic, STFT) sit exactly at those hazards, and c250b05 preserved them rather than moving arithmetic to f16. One caveat on INT8: those reports use ONNX Runtime dynamic quantization, and the S10e does expose `asimddp`, so a tract SDOT path with the STFT convolutions excluded is untested, not refuted. The trajectory research's theoretical 15-25% gain from INT8 is a hypothesis; the external evidence makes it a late, quality-gated experiment.

On cost structure, no independent per-op Kokoro profile exists, so "convolution GEMM dominates" rests on the local profile plus HiFi-GAN-class analogy. A cross-note check, though, is reassuring: the Arm Cortex-A76 optimization guide lists FMLA throughput 2 per cycle ([Arm A76 SOG](https://documentation-service.arm.com/static/5ed4bd67ca06a95ce53f917d)), which yields the 193.5 GFLOP/s three-core FP16 peak assumed in the profile analysis, against 125.8 GFLOP/s measured, the 65% figure. (The guide does not split FP16 from FP32 FMLA rates, so the FP16 peak remains an inference.) For the little cores, the A55 guide shows Q-form FMLA issuing once per cycle and a 64-bit load path ([Arm A55 SOG](https://documentation-service.arm.com/static/641a1d258df5201251be7270)), and ncnn documents that hand-tuned A55 kernels use 64-bit loads to dual-issue ([ncnn](https://ncnn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/developer-guide/arm-a53-a55-dual-issue.html)). That is a real hardware rationale for 5c79700's A55 tile. It is not an energy rationale: the Linux scheduler documentation states that little CPUs "aren't always necessarily more energy-efficient" and that high little OPPs can be worse than low big OPPs ([EAS docs](https://docs.kernel.org/scheduler/sched-energy.html)), and no A55-versus-A76 energy-per-task data exists for the Snapdragon 855. Stage 1 on littles is justified as latency overlap, not as an evidenced energy optimization.

The sharpest external finding is upstream overlap. The fork is vendored at tract 0.22.3 while 0.23.9 shipped on 2026-10-15 ([tract changelog](https://github.com/sonos/tract/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)). Upstream 0.23.0 skips the dispatcher for tiny matmuls via a 64-panel threshold, with native macOS aarch64 effect "net-neutral within +/-2%" ([PR #2185](https://github.com/sonos/tract/pull/2185)); 0.23.5 replaced the kernel picker with a per-architecture cost model that has Cortex-A53/A55 seed classes and a `hwbench` calibration command; 0.23.8 added an integer `NearestUpsample` op ([PR #2829](https://github.com/sonos/tract/pull/2829)); and 0.23.9 fuses elementwise binary chains. No upstream change covers AdaIN-with-Snake fusion, f16-boundary Cast fusion, or Kokoro's half-pixel linear Resizes, and there is no A76 seed class. So commits 3-5 are partly duplicated, commit 2 is the same class of optimization but distinct, and a rebase could shrink the roughly 2,500 fork lines (+2,543/-207 against the vendoring commit); that is inference, because nobody has benchmarked 0.23.x on a Kryo 485, and the vendored tree was not diffed against crates.io 0.22.3. The repo's own "tract version bump" lead has sat unexecuted ([fp16-einsum.md](/data/data/com.termux/files/home/PRJ/kukuryku/docs/fp16-einsum.md)).

On measurement practice, the local protocol is good where it can be: tract's maintainers also interleave binaries, after finding that blocked ordering moved the same binary about 7% ([PR #2829](https://github.com/sonos/tract/pull/2829)). Where it cannot be good is energy. Perfetto documents that fuel-gauge current is an averaged counter, that a USB-connected phone may hold a wakelock so it "will never fully suspend", and that on-device power rails are unavailable on most production phones ([Perfetto](https://perfetto.dev/docs/data-sources/battery-counters)). Research-grade phone energy work uses a Monsoon monitor with the battery bypassed and screen off ([arXiv 2507.02135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02135)). Absolute speed is competitive but not comparable: sherpa-onnx Kokoro v0.19 (precision unstated) on an Exynos Galaxy S10 measured median RTF 1.133 ([VoicePing](https://voiceping-landingpage-prod.pages.dev/en/blog/research-offline-tts-eval/)), against 0.62 wall RTF at HEAD, but SoC, protocol and model differ. The same LiteRT report shows GPU FP16 vocoding gaining about 2x over four CPU threads on newer phones; whether Adreno 640 is reachable from Termux is unknown.

## What is left is GEMM at 65% of peak, and the micro-op pool is nearly spent

Per-node profiles at HEAD (long sentence, instrumented wall time, derived from local git-ignored files in `tools/bench_out`) show where the five commits landed. In stage 2, OptMatMul is unchanged in seconds (6.38 s to 6.30 s) and now 78.0% of the stage; AdaIN-plus-Snake is 8.9%, Cast fell from 7.9% to 3.0%, Resize from 2.8% to 0.4%, and Scan from 1.9% to 1.3%, with about 7% in untouched small ops. Stage 1 shrank 19-31% in op time. The commits therefore harvested the roughly 27% of stage 2 that was not GEMM, and perfectly eliminating what is left outside GEMM would save at most **about 12% of gold CPU**, with realistic recovery of 2-5%. That arithmetic explains the declining slope of per-commit gold-CPU gains (about 0.4%, 0% by design, 4.0%, 1.4%) and why it cannot recover.

The GEMM floor is firm. Stage 2's 74 FP16 convolutions on `16x8_gen` do 746 GFLOP in 5.93 s; the largest group (M=128, K=1408, N=70,201) is 57% of GEMM FLOPs and 42% of stage-2 time. Predicted core-seconds from FLOPs (1.25 per audio-second) match measured gold CPU (1.29), so gold energy tracks FLOPs. The tile question is closed by data: `16x8_gen` beat five alternative tiles on all 11 representative convolutions, and a fourth gold thread bought only about 4% speed for about 7% more CPU, which suggests memory or synchronization limits rather than raw FMA throughput (inference). That also tempers hopes for INT8: a theoretical 2x SDOT ratio will not be realised on a kernel already short of peak for non-FMA reasons.

Energy per audio-second can now move only by fewer FLOPs, cheaper FLOPs or less idle waste. Cheaper FLOPs means a lower gold voltage and frequency (the repo's own battery study calls this "the only remaining lever with a V^2 payoff"), INT8, or moving work to little cores. Fewer FLOPs means model changes such as ablating `noise_res.1` (17.7% of stage 2) and `noise_res.0` (6.4%), a quality decision with no tract work. Warm wall RTF of about 0.62 leaves about 38% spare gold-core time to spend on any of these. Stage 1 matters mostly for first-audio latency now: it runs on one little core, is hidden behind stage 2 on paragraphs, and its remaining costs (Softmax at 8.7% of stage 1, LSTM Scan at 21%) are cheap seconds. A last cost is carrying the fork: about 89.7% of the 43,885 lines added by the five commits are profiling data, and production inference logic is only about 1,250 lines.

The Resize and tiny-dispatch families should not be extended, and the shelved parallel Cast patch stays shelved unless latency, not energy, becomes the goal.

## Conclusion

The series' most reusable output is its infrastructure, not its optimizations: the bit-exact raw-tensor parity gate, the warm interleaved A/B harness with same-executable controls, and opt-out environment switches let each change be accepted or reverted on evidence. What the series exposes is a mismatch between metric and objective. CPU seconds are cluster-blind, so the largest measured gain (5c79700) is the cheapest silicon, and wall-time profile shares overstated what fusion could save in work because serial time is not CPU time. The project's own accept/shelve rule then operated below its noise floor. The four optimization commits each state that energy is unmeasured; the conclusion that follows is that the series has proven a 10% CPU and 6% gold-CPU reduction and nothing about the battery.

Two things change the plan. First, the lowest-clock experiment is large enough to be seen with today's noisy meter, so the measurement fix and the frequency test can proceed together, and together they decide whether further tract work is worth doing at all. Second, a stock tract 0.23.9 spike should precede any new tract-level patch, because upstream has already moved on kernel selection, dispatch thresholds and elementwise fusion, and the cheapest way to lower fork cost is to find out which of the five commits it still needs. If the stock spike shows the fork retains a large lead, the micro-op work has earned its keep; if not, retiring parts of commits 3-5 is the highest-value cleanup available.
